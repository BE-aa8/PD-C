Artificial-Pancreas Control Simulator

Welcome to Closing the Loop, an interactive web-based process dynamics and control simulator designed for 4th-year chemical engineering undergraduates. This application models the automated regulation of blood glucose concentration in an illustrative Type 1 diabetic individual using a digital continuous glucose monitor (CGM) and a continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) pump.

Core Textbook Case: Based on Process Dynamics and Control (Seborg, Edgar, Mellichamp & Doyle, 4th ed., Example 23.3 & Exercise 23.6). The physiological engine uses the three-compartment minimal model of glucose-insulin kinetics originally developed by Bergman and adapted by Bequette (2002).

Chemical Engineering Concepts Explored

  • Physiological Dynamics & Basal Equilibrium: Non-zero basal state, non-symmetric actuator authority (pump cannot remove insulin), and nonlinear steady-state gain.
  • Linearization & Model Reduction: Linearizing nonlinear differential equations into 3rd-order transfer functions; reduction via the Skogestad half rule and least-squares fitting into SOPDT and FOPDT models.
  • Internal Model Control (IMC) Synthesis: Analytical tuning of discrete PID controllers across various reduced models with selectable closed-loop filter time constants (τc).
  • Feedback Loop Hazards: Discrete sampling period (Ts = 5 min), measurement delay (θs = 10 min), subcutaneous lag (τs), and anti-windup clamping vs. back-calculation.
  • Dynamic Disturbance Rejection: Meal carbohydrates modelled as an exponential decay disturbance; feedforward pre-bolus scheduling and improper lead-lag transfer functions.
  • Frequency-Domain Robustness: Loop gain, phase margins (with and without delay), gain margins, and maximum tolerable latency before closed-loop instability.
  • Advanced Architectures: Benchmark comparison against precomputed Model Predictive Control (MPC) trajectories under unannounced and announced meal scenarios.

Why Real Systems Are Harder (Qualitative Process Insights)

1. Instantaneous Meal Absorption

In this minimal model, carbohydrate disturbance appearance D(t) begins immediately at meal onset. In real human physiology, gastric emptying and intestinal absorption introduce an additional 15 to 45 minute asymmetric dynamic lag before glucose enters the bloodstream.

2. Lumped Subcutaneous Kinetics

The model lumps subcutaneous insulin infusion directly into blood plasma clearance. Real insulin analogs require hexamer dissociation and capillary transport, adding pharmacokinetic delays that restrict high-bandwidth feedback control.

3. Absence of Counter-Regulation

The textbook minimal model lacks pancreatic alpha-cell counter-regulation (glucagon release). When glucose drops below 40 mg/dL, real physiology activates hepatic glycogenolysis or emergency glucose recovery; here, severe hypoglycemia persists without rescue.

Minimal Model Mathematics & Linearization

The system dynamics are described by Bergman's physiological minimal model in deviation variables, modified for an absolute insulin infusion rate U(t). Every calculation below is documented with its mathematical formula, units, and physical interpretation.

Blood Glucose Deviation Dynamics
State: G(t) [mg/dL] Rate: mg/dL/min

Rate of change of glucose deviation balances insulin-independent uptake (p1), insulin-dependent uptake scaled by remote compartment action X, and meal carbohydrate appearance D(t).

Remote Insulin Compartment Dynamics
State: X(t) [1/min] Rate: 1/min²

Remote interstitial insulin activity builds up in response to plasma insulin deviation I, decaying with first-order time constant τX = 1/p2.

Plasma Insulin Deviation Dynamics
State: I(t) [mU/L] Rate: mU/(L•min)

Plasma insulin deviation reflects the net balance between pump infusion deviation (U - Ubasal) diluted into distribution volume V1 and first-order hepatic clearance n.

Basal Steady-State Infusion
Equilibrium: dI/dt = 0 Units: mU/min

The continuous basal pump delivery rate required to maintain fasting basal insulin concentration Ib = 15 mU/L in the absence of disturbance.

Actuator Authority Asymmetry
Range: [-16.7, +98.3] Dimensionless

The pump cannot remove insulin (Umin = 0), yielding only 16.7 mU/min downward control authority compared to 98.3 mU/min upward authority (severe asymmetry).

Ideal Feedforward Improper Transfer Function
Ideal Controller: Gf(s) Improper (Degree 2)

Perfect disturbance cancellation requires two pure derivative lead terms with zero lags, making the ideal feedforward controller physically unrealizable without approximations.

Multi-Model Artificial Intelligence Workflow

This educational simulator demonstrates a full multi-model AI development lifecycle adhering to course specifications:
